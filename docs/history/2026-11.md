## Phase 6 so far

- **Built:** frame rates, gyro logging, the 180° button, PCM audio and native gamuts.
- **Color space:** a picker in Settings under Image, always shown. Each choice names what it pairs
  with, so Apple Log's lack of a gamut of its own shows by omission. Choosing a gamut other than
  Rec.2020 sets its curve. The saved `nativeGamut` setting then follows the curve: Apple Log falls
  back to Rec.2020, and S-Log3 or LogC3 return to their own gamut. `Gamut.fromRec2020` builds each
  matrix from published primaries, and the pipeline multiplies it after the DNG matrix, which adds
  no GPU work. The Rec.709 monitor LUT and still export follow the setting. Sidecars and
  `still.json` name the gamut, and the file's own tag stays nominal Rec.2020 because it can't name
  these gamuts. The `.cube` conversion LUTs receive the recorded gamut, and stills keep the file
  name `log_rec2020.png` either way.
- **Needs samples first:** vignette correction needs a real `reference.dng` to find the
  `FixVignetteRadial` values, and highlight reconstruction needs clips with clipped highlights.
  Color accuracy needs a chart. Any real-time GPU work then needs a frame-cost test.
- **Needs the phone:** lens tests, the stabilization check and focus while recording.
- **Focus peaking sensitivity:** Low uses a threshold of 0.15 and High uses 0.075 since 2026-11-07,
  between the first 0.1 and 0.05 and the later 0.2 and 0.1, set in Settings under Viewfinder. It's a
  `MonitorParams` field, so it adds no GPU work. Untested on the phone.
- **Settings pauses the stream:** opening Settings stops the stream, unless a take is running, and
  closing it resumes. Opening Diagnostics resumes it for good, because its tools need it. Looks and
  LUTs therefore can't be previewed behind the sheet. Untested on the phone.
- **Not started:** shutter priority, which needs an ISO loop during capture and its own frame-cost
  test, auto exposure with an EV slider, external microphones and file tagging options.

- **Test link:** stream control, the settings and actions above, a preview still, RAW clip fetching,

## New in `623c5a9`, untested on the phone

- **Decimal rates:** see the session above: the fix below is right, but the camera delivers 24 and 30.
  `VideoEncoder` never set `videoInput.mediaTimeScale`, so the track probably got the default of 600.
  At that scale a 23.98 frame lasts 25.025 ticks and a 29.97 frame 20.02, so both rounded to whole
  rates, which matches what Resolve showed. `FrameRates.mediaTimeScale` now gives 1000 ticks per
  whole frame per second, 24000 for 23.98 and 30000 for 29.97, so a decimal frame lasts exactly 1001
  ticks. A test writes 23.98 and 29.97 files on the simulator and reads back the track's timescale
  and rate. Timestamps are still each frame's real capture time, so Resolve may show a rate that
  isn't exactly 23.976 if capture jitters.
- **Presets:** the save button reads **Save current settings**. **Reset all settings** asks in a
  centered alert instead of a sheet from the bottom. A row's menu has **Update to current
  settings**, which keeps the name, place and default, and is disabled when the preset already
  matches.
- **Curve names:** short names follow each brand's spelling in capitals: S-LOG3, S-LOG2, LOGC3,
  LOGC4, C-LOG3. Apple has no short name, so ALOG and ALOG2 stay. The owner asked for "S-LOG 3" as
  the example, but Sony writes S-Log3 without a space.
- **New curves:** S-Log2 with S-Gamut, LogC4 with ARRI Wide Gamut 4, and Canon Log 3 (v1.2) with
  Cinema Gamut. Formulas and primaries come from colour-science's `develop` branch, and the tests
  check colour-science's own examples: middle gray 0.3395 for S-Log2, 0.2784 for LogC4 and 0.3434
  for Canon Log 3. S-Gamut has the same primaries as S-Gamut3. Cinema Gamut's green has y 1.14, as
  Canon publishes. The shader switches on raw values 4, 5 and 6, and settings save the label. Lists
  are grouped by brand. Grading any of them hasn't been checked.
- **Pair curve and color space:** a switch in Settings under Image, on by default and kept in
  presets. While on, every curve records its own gamut: choosing a curve chooses its gamut, choosing
  a gamut chooses its curve, and Rec.2020 chooses Apple Log. Off, it behaves as before, where any
  curve but Apple Log 2 can record Rec.2020, and turning it off keeps the current pair. The link
  sets it with `set pairsGamut`. An older saved setup with S-Log3 or LogC3 in Rec.2020 now opens in
  S-Gamut3 or ARRI Wide Gamut 3 unless the switch is turned off.
- **ProRes 4444 at 12 bits:** it replaces the 10-bit 4444 under the same id and label, so saved
  settings and presets keep it. The encoder now gets 16-bit AYCbCr
  (`kCVPixelFormatType_4444AYpCbCr16`) in one plane, with opaque alpha, so ProRes keeps its 12 bits
  of color instead of 10. The 10-bit version gave nothing over ProRes 422 HQ but chroma, at about
  60% more data. New kernels `encodePacked` (Maximum) and `processDirectPacked` (Efficient and the
  hot path) write it with the same math, keeping each code's fraction. `EncoderPreset.bitDepth`
  gives 12 for it, and the `Encoding` line and sidecar's `bitDepth` record it. The encoder benchmark
  now fills packed buffers. Tests check that both paths agree, that a code of 400.25 comes out as
  12-bit 1601, and that a ProRes 4444 file on the simulator keeps steps finer than 10 bits. Whether
  the phone's encoder accepts this format at 4032x3024 and 30 fps is unknown: there's no fallback,
  so a refusal would show as a writer failure. The frame buffer grows from 6 to 8 bytes a pixel, so
  watch copy, GPU and memory figures against take `185616`.
- **Apple's preview hidden:** `SessionPreview` is hidden whenever the Metal monitor shows, so the
  bright Apple picture no longer flashes at launch. It stays connected, so it saves no load. With
  the **ISP preview** look, it shows as before, including during a take.
- **Disconnect Apple's preview:** now the normal behavior, see **After `fa15863`**. It was a
  Diagnostics switch, off at every launch and kept out of presets. While VibeLog's own viewfinder
  shows, it takes the hidden preview off the session. It can't change during a take, and the
  connection holds until the take ends. The sidecar's `appPreview`, the link's status and the heat
  log record whether it was connected. Whether photo capture or auto exposure depends on it is
  unknown, so it needs a short take before a long one. If it saves heat, the end goal is to make
  this the normal behavior and drop the switch: **ISP preview** stays under **Look**, the preview
  connects only while that look is chosen, and any other look disconnects it.
- **Suites:** `tools/suites/hot_switches.json` runs the two long tests below, and
  `tools/suites/app_preview.json`, since removed, ran a long take with Apple's preview disconnected.
  Both match `222321`'s setup: 1x, Maximum quality, uncompressed audio, correction off and all tools
  except zebras.

## New in `c459497`, untested on the phone

- **Owner's checks on `7501cae`:** idle pause, the black launch, the curve names in the LOG wheel,
  the library turning, the **Advanced** page and the centered reset popup all work.
- **23.98 and 29.97 removed:** the camera delivered 24 and 30 either way. `FrameRates.all` is 24, 25

  Diagnostics button and the link's `zoomProbe` are gone.
- **S-Gamut3.Cine:** added, from primaries R (0.766, 0.275), G (0.225, 0.800), B (0.089, -0.087),
  D65, and now S-Log3's default. S-Gamut3 stays, and still records S-Log3.
- **Curve and color space stored together:** `ProcessingSettings` holds the curve and the gamut
  directly. With pairing on, choosing a curve sets its gamut from `GamutPairing`, and choosing a
  gamut sets its curve, so S-Gamut3 and S-Gamut3.Cine can both record S-Log3. Off, each changes
  alone. `GamutPairing.gamuts(for:)` and `curves(for:)` keep Apple Wide Gamut for Apple Log 2 alone,

  each curve, a curve for each color space, and a viewfinder LUT for each curve in its paired color
  space. Kept in presets as `curveGamuts`, `gamutCurves` and `curveLUTs`, with only the changes from
  the standard pairs saved. A LUT is keyed by curve and gamut together, because a conversion LUT
  expects both. Choosing a pair selects its LUT, a LUT linked to the last pair is cleared, and
  choosing a LUT linked to exactly one pair chooses that pair, outside takes. Reading settings back
  sets off none of these. Editing the current curve's row moves the gamut with it.
- **12-bit ProRes 4444 is a switch:** the 4444 preset is 10-bit again, and **12-bit ProRes 4444**
  under **Advanced** feeds it 16 bits when on, off by default and kept in presets.
  `EncoderPreset.twelveBit` and `AppModel.takePreset` do it. The link sets it with
  `twelveBitProRes`.
- **12-bit HEVC trial:** the encoder test also runs ProRes 4444 and HEVC 4:4:4 from 16-bit input,
  writes 12-bit detail into the frames, reads the first frame back, and reports `kept 12 bits` or
  `kept 10 bits`. Run it with the link's `encoderTest` and read the `Encoder test:` lines. A
  **12-bit HEVC** switch follows only if HEVC keeps 12.
- **Viewfinder math on the GPU:** the Rec.709 look no longer uses the 33-point LUT, whose error
  differed by curve, up to 19/255 for LogC4. `monitorDisplay` in the shader decodes the curve,
  converts the gamut to Rec.709 and applies the same highlight roll-off per pixel, matching
  `MonitorTransform.display`, and a GPU test checks the two agree within one 8-bit step for every
  curve. It's a few more operations per drawable pixel, so check `draw` time in a take against
  earlier figures, about 6 ms. A custom `.cube` still goes through its own LUT. The look is now
  called **Rec.709**.

  ports OpenColorIO's ACES 2.0 output transform from its C++, `ACES13.swift` ports the Academy's
  ACES 1.3 reference CTL, and `BuiltInLook.swift` adds the Rec.709 view around them as OpenColorIO's

## Batch 7, FX in the viewfinder and export, built as `f53121e`

- **Tab:** FX, a fourth tab beside ADJUST and COLOR, hiding with them for LOG: HAL, BLOOM, DIFF, VIG
  and GRAIN, one slider at a time with the same draft, haptics and tap-again reset, and RESET FX at
  the bottom. Halation, bloom, diffusion and grain go 0 to 100, vignette -100 to 100, where below
  zero lightens the corners. `LookEffects` in `VLCore`, in `AppConfig` and presets, and put back by
  Cancel. Not in styles yet.
- **How:** effects aren't in the LUT, since they need neighbours. `EffectsKit` makes an eighth-size
  copy of the log image in linear light, sixteen taps per texel, then blurs it both ways with a
  25-tap Gaussian, sigma six texels, about 50 sensor pixels at full size. `applyEffects` in the
  shader then works per pixel in linear light in the recorded gamut: diffusion mixes toward the
  blurred copy and adds its highlights, highlights being what's above 0.6, about 1.7 stops over
  middle gray; bloom adds them neutral; halation adds them tinted red-orange (1, 0.32, 0.12);
  vignette scales by distance from the centre; and grain adds noise that follows the square root of
  luminance and moves each frame. The result is encoded back to the curve, then the look runs as
  usual, so FX work under every look, imported LUTs included.
- **Viewfinder:** `monitorFragment` applies them per drawn pixel with the glow from the same command
  buffer, so a drag shows at once with no LUT to build. Zebras and false color still judge the
  picture before the effects, and the scopes measure it before them too. Cost while on: the
  downsample and blur each draw, estimated about 1 to 2 ms, unmeasured; nothing while off.
- **Export:** `LookExporter.Look.effects` runs the glow and `effectsApply` on each full-size frame
  before the look, in the preview player and the export alike, and the export's sidecar names them.
